## 1. ChessReD (Masouris & van Gemert, "End-to-End Chess Recognition", arXiv 2310.04086, VISAPP 2024)

Primary links: paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04086 · dataset https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/99b5c721-280b-450b-b058-b2900b69a90f (DOI 10.4121/99b5c721-280b-450b-b058-b2900b69a90f.v2) · code https://github.com/tmasouris/end-to-end-chess-recognition (the `ThanosM97/...` URL redirects here).

- **Size / games / split.** "100 chess games, each with an arbitrary number of moves and therefore images, amounting to a total of 10,800 images"; split 60/20/20 → "6,479 training images, 2,192 validation images, and 2,129 test images" **[via search, 4TU page + paper]**. The ChessQueries loader hard-codes the same counts: `CHESSRED: TRAIN 6_479, VAL 2_192, TEST 2_129` **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/chessqueries/data/inventory.py
- **Capture protocol.** Positions "drawn from openings in the Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings"; "the selected games played out on a physical chessboard with images captured after each move" **[via search, 4TU page]**.
- **Phones.** 4TU page: "Three distinct smartphone models were used to capture the images. Each model has different camera specifications, such as resolution and sensor type." **[via search]** The specific models (iPhone 12, Huawei P40 Pro, Samsung Galaxy S8, ~3024–3072 px square) appear only in a secondary summary (https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/chess-recognition-dataset-chessred) which claims the paper names them in its stratified-split description; not confirmed against the paper here.
- **Camera angles / viewpoints (the key quote).** 4TU page: "The images were also taken from diverse angles, ranging from top-view to oblique angles, and from different perspectives (e.g., white player perspective, side view, etc.)." **[via search]** Paper: side views are described as simulating "a bystander's viewpoint"; and "Occlusions between pieces occur more often in images captured from a low angle or a player's perspective, while pieces are rarely occluded in top-view images"; also that distinguishing features "(e.g. the queen's crown) ... are less distinguishable in a top-view" **[via search, arXiv 2310.04086]**. The GitHub README's sample-image captions are exactly: "Corner view", "Player view", "Low angle", "Top view" **[direct]** https://github.com/tmasouris/end-to-end-chess-recognition#readme
- **No numeric elevation range or distance is given** in any primary text found. The "0° to 60°+" range circulating online comes only from the Emergent Mind summary, not the paper or 4TU page.
- **Design stance.** Paper: the approach "does not impose restrictions such as specific angles and orientations" and "we do not rely on user input, or specific viewing angles"; it criticises Chesscog because "the user must manually input the specific player's perspective" **[via search]**.
- **Corner annotations: yes, for a subset ("ChessReD2K").** Paper/4TU v2: "bounding box and chessboard corner annotations only for a subset of 20 randomly selected games (2,078 images)"; split 70/15/15 by game → 14 train games (1,442 images), 3 val (330), 3 test (306), stratified across the three cameras. Corners are labelled "bottom-left, bottom-right, top-left, top-right" relative to the white player, so board orientation can be inferred **[via search; sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04086 and https://data.4tu.nl/datasets/99b5c721-280b-450b-b058-b2900b69a90f/2]**. Note: the official `chessred.py` browser reads only `annotations.pieces`, `categories`, `images` — it never loads corners **[direct]** https://github.com/tmasouris/end-to-end-chess-recognition/blob/main/chessred.py
- **Baseline numbers (paper, Table).** ResNeXt end-to-end: board accuracy 15.26%, mean incorrect squares/board 3.40; Chesscog on ChessReD: ~2.3% boards correct, 42.87 incorrect squares/board, and "Chesscog could not infer board orientation, so the authors supplied all orientations during evaluation" **[via search, arXiv 2310.04086]**. ChessQueries later cites this as "15.3%" and "~2%" https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30762.
- **Licence conflict.** Paper text says "openly accessible at 4TU.ResearchData Data Repository, under CC BY 4.0 license", but the 4TU record page itself links `creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/` **[via search]**. Check the 4TU page before commercial use.
- **Browse sample images:** README gallery at https://github.com/tmasouris/end-to-end-chess-recognition#readme (four captioned photos); full files at the 4TU record above; pre-resized 1024×1024 set linked from the README (Google Drive) **[direct]**.

## 2. ChessQueries (J. Seytre, arXiv 2608.30762; github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries; HF joelseytre/chessqueries, joelseytre/chessq-lite)

- **Input: raw photo, no corners.** Paper: the whole board is produced "in one forward pass, with no required intermediate output such as board detection or corner estimation" **[via search]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30762. CLI is `chessqueries-predict photo.jpg` **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries. Architecture: "a 304M-parameter ViT-L/14 encoder that maps a 644 × 644 input image to patch tokens. Sixty-four learned queries—one for each board square—cross-attend to those tokens through a DETR-style decoder." **[direct, README]**
- **Orientation / side views.** Paper: the approach "handles inputs of any orientation, whether seen from one of the players' perspectives, or from the side" **[via search]**. Limitation stated: "in certain positions the model can be confused about the direction in which the white and black pawns move" (Kasparov–Topalov example), and "in a practical real-world setup, it would be helpful to train the model only on images where there is a clear signal, such as the SLCC data, where the white-side player is always on the left" **[via search]**. Footnote: prior ChessReD results "took the best results of all possible board orientations" **[via search]**. Lichess integration thread: a tester noted "it doesn't translate perspective (ie its blacks turn orientation)" and the author acknowledged the model didn't handle that yet **[direct]** https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/pull/21908. The training help text says "'geometric'/'geometric_mild' add rotation/perspective/scale jitter for viewpoint robustness ... (labels are viewpoint-invariant)" **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/scripts/train_chessqueries.py
- **Accuracy by camera angle: NOT reported as a table.** Headline exact-board accuracies (README): ChessReD 99.5%, ChessCog 98.5%, SLCC 87.1%, CVChess (zero-shot) 87.6% **[direct]**. The evaluation script's only breakdown is `--subset-by resolution` (crop pixel area); `SUBSET_FEATURES = {"resolution": _resolution}` — no viewpoint banding **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/scripts/eval_cross_dataset.py. The repo does ship per-image viewpoint labels for CVChess: `cvchess_viewpoints.json` keyed by `white_side`, with a `Viewpoint` enum of 8 cells of a 3×3 grid ("Where the WHITE player sits in the photo"; "BOTTOM is the standard white-player view") **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/chessqueries/data/cvchess.py. Qualitative viewpoint findings in the paper: an appendix figure of "CVChess' hard viewpoints (for a human): every board is exact", while the four worst CVChess failures "appear from the default viewpoint, facing the white side" **[via search]**.
- **Augmentations (exact code).** `Augment.GEOMETRIC_MILD: dict(rotation=45, perspective=0.3, scale=(0.85, 1.1))`; `Augment.GEOMETRIC: dict(rotation=180, perspective=0.5, scale=(0.7, 1.1))`; `RandomRotation(g["rotation"])` applied with p=0.9; `RandomPerspective(distortion_scale=g["perspective"], p=0.7)`; `RandomAffine(degrees=0, translate=(0.08, 0.08), scale=g["scale"])`; `ColorJitter(brightness=0.2, contrast=0.2, saturation=0.2)`; no flips, no hue **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/chessqueries/data/transforms.py. Default is `GEOMETRIC_MILD` **[direct, train_chessqueries.py]**. Paper wording: "mild geometric transforms (random rotation up to 45°, perspective distortion, scaling in [0.85,1.1] and small translations) together with light color jitter" **[via search]**. Ablation: removing geometric augmentation drops CVChess to "41.2% board accuracy, 6.6 wrong squares" because "that dataset presents extreme view angles"; ViT-B instead of ViT-L drops CVChess 87.6% → 51.4% **[via search]**.
- **SLCC and CVChess.** SLCC: "2,174 images from 20 Saint Louis Chess Club / Grand Chess Tour broadcast videos"; difficulty "coming from camera angle, lighting, board and piece set, background clutter and player/hand occlusions" **[via search]**; loader docstring "SLCC broadcast dataset: human-reviewed frames reconstructed from relay videos", per-frame fields include `crop_bbox`, `side_to_move`, `gt_fen` — no corners **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/chessqueries/data/slcc.py; counts 1,475/326/373 **[direct, inventory.py]**; annotations CC BY-NC 4.0, frames not distributed **[direct, README]**. CVChess: 352 images, one game, eval-only (`splits = ()`), FENs vendored in `cvchess_labels.json` **[direct]**; origin is the CVChess paper (Kasparov–Topalov 1999 re-played, each position shot from "a standard top-down view and four additional perspectives around the board") **[via search]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11522.
- **Few-shot / LoRA: yes.** `scripts/lora_fewshot.py` — "Few-shot domain adaptation of a trained recognizer: LoRA vs head-, encoder- and full-FT"; args `--k` (support size), `--rank 8 --alpha 16 --dropout 0.05`, `--steps 1500`, `--lr` per mode; `LORA_TARGETS = ("attn.qkv", "attn.proj", "mlp.fc1", "mlp.fc2")`; `DEFAULT_LR` LORA 1e-4, HEAD_FT 2e-4, ENCODER_FT/FULL_FT 5e-6 **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/scripts/lora_fewshot.py, https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/chessqueries/models/adapt.py. **Data format:** no generic image+FEN folder loader — a target must be a registered `ChessDataset` with TRAIN and VAL splits ("one needs a TRAIN split to draw the support set"), non-empty TEST, and samples are `BoardSample(image_path, board, dataset, sample_id, split, meta)` where `board` is built from a FEN/placement; `load_support` subsamples TRAIN to k images **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/chessqueries/data/fewshot.py, .../data/base.py. Paper result: LoRA on 10 SLCC frames matches the full-data ResNeXt baseline (26.0%) **[via search]**.
- **Square localisation: yes, via attention.** `viz/attention.py` extracts decoder "query->patch cross-attention", `cross[i]` shaped (B, heads, 64, N_tokens); "Query index == square index in FEN order (a8=0 .. h1=63), by construction"; `cross_grid()` → (B, 64, H, W) maps; `cross_centroids` → one (x, y) per square in normalised [0,1]² coordinates for a "query->square alignment plot" **[direct]** https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/main/chessqueries/viz/attention.py. Paper: "the attention is trained to focus accurately on each respective square" and under occlusion "focuses on the parts of the piece that appear in-between the surrounding pieces" **[via search]**.
- **ChessQ Lite.** HF card: "Release weights for ChessQ Lite V4, a smaller chessboard recognizer further trained on proprietary data"; one file, "ViT-S FP16 weights (63.15 MB)" safetensors; PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 **[via search]** https://huggingface.co/joelseytre/chessq-lite. Lichess runs it client-side: "a pure client implementation using the onnxruntime-web package"; "The int8 quantized version running on CPU actually outperforms the larger fp16 model" **[direct]** https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/pull/21908.
- **Licences.** Code and paper weights PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0; "Commercial use is not licensed." **[direct, README]**

## 3. Synthetic data: chesscog (Wölflein & Arandjelović 2021) and later 3D work

- **Tool and size.** Paper (J. Imaging 7(6):94, arXiv 2104.14963): 4,888 positions (2% sample of 2,851 Carlsen games, duplicates removed), split 90/3/7 **[via search]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14963; dataset DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/XF3KA, whose page says the images were "rendered in Blender" **[via search]** https://osf.io/xf3ka/wiki/home/. README: "~5,000 synthetically generated images ... 3D renderings of different chess positions at various camera angles and lighting conditions" **[direct]** https://github.com/georg-wolflein/chesscog. ChessQueries loader counts 4,400/146/342 **[direct]**.
- **Camera randomisation.** "viewing angles between 45° and 60° to the board" (ChessReD paper characterises this as "a limited range of viewing angles ... images taken only from the players' perspectives"); lighting "either randomly oriented spotlights or a simulated camera flash"; pieces "randomly rotated around their vertical axis and offset from square centres using a normal distribution"; labels are FEN plus "the pixel coordinates of the board's four corners" **[via search, arXiv 2104.14963 / 2310.04086]**. No distance/azimuth range was found in the retrievable text.
- **Few-shot adaptation.** "adapt the inference system to a previously unseen chess set using just two photos of the starting position", reaching "per-square accuracy of 99.83%" on that set; board localisation reused unchanged, the occupancy and piece CNNs fine-tuned with heavy augmentation **[via search]**. Release notes claim ">99.9% per-square accuracy ... using only two training images of the starting position from both players' perspectives" https://github.com/georg-wolflein/chesscog/releases. The script `chesscog.transfer_learning.create_dataset` "Adds corner annotations to the JSON labels ... using corner detection" then builds occupancy/ and pieces/ crops **[direct]** https://github.com/georg-wolflein/chesscog/blob/master/chesscog/transfer_learning/create_dataset.py. Independent re-test (Faster R-CNN paper): 88.89% board / 0.17% per-square error on unseen set **[via search]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09191.
- **Other 3D-rendered work (2024–2026).** (a) *Unsupervised Domain Adaptation Approaches for Chessboard Recognition* (arXiv 2410.15206): Blender script randomly assigning pieces A1–H8, lighting randomised, "camera stays fixed above the board", 4,500 images **[via search]**. (b) *BYO-Eval* (arXiv 2506.05440): Blender Python API with "control over blur, camera distance and angle, table shape and texture, light or resolution", chess among the scenes — for VLM evaluation, not recognition training **[via search]**. (c) TU Delft MSc thesis (Waterhout, 2024): 3D-scanned pieces, "61,910 synthetic images with domain randomization" for 6D pose, robotics not board state **[via search]** https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_493677ee-def4-4c0d-b09d-32dbcaf96ea6. (d) ChessRender360 (Zenodo 13374802): "10,000 rendered chess positions" with depth, masks, boxes, board corners, CC BY 4.0 — no accompanying paper found **[via search]** https://zenodo.org/records/13374802. (e) ChessSight corner heatmap (HF): "trained only on synthetic renders" (see §4). (f) Sim-to-real gap evidence: a third-party PR states "the existing chesscog-trained `corner_heatmap.pth` transfers poorly to real photos (synthetic-to-real domain gap)" **[direct]** https://github.com/NikolaKujoh/chess-game-to-fen/pull/3.
- **NeRF / Gaussian splatting for chess:** no paper, dataset or repo found in any search.

## 4. Automatic board-corner detection

- **chesscog (classical).** Call order in `find_corners`: `resize_image` → `_detect_edges` (Canny, `cfg.EDGE_DETECTION`) → `_detect_lines` (Hough, with `DIAGONAL_LINE_ELIMINATION`) → `_cluster_horizontal_and_vertical_lines` → `_eliminate_similar_lines` → `_get_intersection_points` → RANSAC loop (`_compute_homography`, `_warp_points`, `_discard_outliers`/`_find_best_scale` with `cfg.RANSAC.OFFSET_TOLERANCE`, `_quantize_points`) → `compute_transformation_matrix` → `_compute_vertical_borders`/`_compute_horizontal_borders` (Sobel border refinement) → `sort_corner_points` **[direct]** https://github.com/georg-wolflein/chesscog/blob/master/chesscog/corner_detection/detect_corners.py. MIT. Paper: Hough "typically yields around 200 lines", RANSAC fits "a projective transformation that maps the board onto a regular grid" **[via search]**. **Failure rate on real photos:** ChessQueries reports ChessCog "board corner detection fails on 65.7% / 96.8% / 50.0% of ChessReD / SLCC / CVChess" **[via search]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30762.
- **Learned keypoints (Govind & Arandjelović, arXiv 2610.09191).** "eight labelled boundary keypoints, predicted with the Mask R-CNN keypoint head, to estimate a homography to an 8x8 grid"; "97.11% fall within 1% of the image diagonal"; full pipeline 76.61% exact boards **[via search]**. No weights/ONNX found.
- **Learned heatmap: ChessSight (HF `tchauffi/chesssight-corner-heatmap-v1`).** Swin-T; "predicts a single corner-likeness map with four peaks, then orders the corners geometrically"; "0.128 squares mean corner error on the real ChessReD test split"; "trained only on synthetic renders"; PyTorch weights, **CC BY-NC 4.0** **[via search]** https://huggingface.co/tchauffi/chesssight-corner-heatmap-v1. Not ONNX; no GitHub repo found.
- **CVChess (arXiv 2511.11522).** Classical: grayscale → Gaussian blur → Canny → "keeps the largest four-corner contour as the board" → warp to 400×400; authors note "Corners shift inward on steep camera angles" **[via search]**.
- **Elucidation/ChessboardDetect** (MIT): Hough + saddle-point CNN classifier (~12k tiles, "roughly 96%"), "a staging ground of half-baked hacky code", no exported weights **[direct]** https://github.com/Elucidation/ChessboardDetect.
- **Browser-ready options.** *fenshot* (MIT): screenshots/diagrams only, "gradient peak analysis" board finder from tensorflow_chessbot, "~330k params, 1.3 MB ONNX ... onnxruntime-web, WASM" — not for photos **[direct]** https://github.com/scoriiu/fenshot. *chess-corners-rs* (MIT): X-junction detector for calibration-style boards, npm `@vitavision/chess-corners` WASM, "optional ONNX refiner (`ml-refiner` feature)", weights not described **[direct]** https://github.com/VitalyVorobyev/chess-corners-rs. *Roboflow Universe* corner models (several, CC BY 4.0 datasets) are hosted-API, no ONNX shown **[via search]**. ChessQ Lite itself runs in-browser via onnxruntime-web (Lichess PR) but is PolyForm NC and does not output corners.
- **Net:** no permissively-licensed, photo-robust, learned corner model with downloadable ONNX weights was found. Closest is ChessSight (NC licence, PyTorch) or exporting a self-trained YOLO keypoint model on ChessReD2K's 2,078 corner-labelled images.

## 5. Camera-angle sensitivity, with numbers where they exist

- **ChessReD paper:** qualitative only — more occlusion at "low angle or a player's perspective", fewer cues in "top-view". No per-angle accuracy table **[via search]**.
- **ChessQueries:** no per-angle table; ablation shows geometric augmentation matters most on the dataset "with extreme view angles" (CVChess 87.6% → 41.2% without it); worst failures are from the *default* white-side view, hard oblique views all exact **[via search]**. ChessCog corner detection fails 65.7–96.8% on real/broadcast angles vs. its 45–60° training range **[via search]**.
- **Xiangqi thesis (K. May, HAW Hamburg):** "For top-down or slight-angle images ... the board is correctly recognised more than 95% of the time, the position in more than 60%"; "for low-angle images and ... backlighting ... the algorithm is not yet robust enough and achieves low accuracy" — no low-angle number **[via search]** https://reposit.haw-hamburg.de/handle/20.500.12738/16128.
- **CVChess:** "accurate on well-lit, front-facing boards. Corners shift inward on steep camera angles, and errors rise with warped views or side lighting"; overall 63.9% **[via search]**.
- **Faster R-CNN paper:** lists camera angle among factors, no split numbers **[via search]**.

- Any accuracy-vs-camera-angle table in ChessReD, ChessQueries, CVChess or the Faster R-CNN paper.
- Whether `cvchess_viewpoints.json` was used to compute per-viewpoint metrics in the ChessQueries paper (only qualitative appendix figures were found).
- Chesscog's camera distance/azimuth randomisation and its board-localisation failure rate on its own synthetic test set.
- ChessSight's architecture details beyond "Swin-T", input size, parameter count, and the synthetic renderer used.
- Any chess-recognition work using NeRF or Gaussian splatting.
- ChessReD's definitive licence (CC BY 4.0 per paper vs CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 per 4TU page).
- ChessQ Lite parameter count and accuracy (card references a "validation summary" not retrieved).
