## The product

A phone on a tripod at one table of a chess club's social evening watches the
board; a web app mirrors the game live, saves PGN, offers a hidden eval bar.
Later: Google sign-in, game history, leaderboard, QR self-join, story card for
Instagram, backend with SQLite on a new small Lighthouse box. Live at
`https://chess.banis.ink`. One table at launch; the owner operates the phone.

Camera setup the owner uses: phone at the side of the board, at the midpoint,
looking down at about 45 degrees, players left and right. That is the hardest
view for occlusion. Target 55 to 65 degrees.

## Architecture in one paragraph

Recognizer (ChessQ Lite, ViT-S, PolyForm Noncommercial) turns one stable photo
into 64x13 per-square probabilities plus 64 attention centroids. A
TypeScript decoder keeps the game state and scores candidate explanations
(no change, legal move, undo, two-ply, illegal) on the changed squares only,
under four rotations, with tier priors and a floor of 1e-3; pending frames wait,
then ask with ranked candidates. The 3D twin (planned) adds per-square
visibility weighting and a silhouette-change term from a cylinder model with
camera pose from the grid's vanishing points, and a beam of three hypotheses.
Stockfish WASM drives the eval bar only. Licences: app GPL-3.0 because of
chessground; weights non-commercial.

## Standing review findings worth remembering

- Measure before tuning; every plan has numeric gates in `eval/RESULTS.md`.
- The decoder must never silently accept a wrong ply; prompts are acceptable,
  silent corruption is not. Gate metric: silent errors 0, prompts at most 5%.
- Promotion defaults to queen; illegal moves truncate the PGN with a comment.
- Two-ply candidates and the pending state are core, not later features.
- Rotation of the model's output can flip between frames; score all four.
- Do not open the dev VM to the internet; production goes on its own box with
  SSH restricted, console terminal as fallback.
